Spinach Disk Photosynthesis Lab

What changes how fast spinach disks float? Light distance, light color, carbon dioxide, and temperature.

Background and directions

Performance expectationHS-LS1-5 Science practicePlanning and carrying out investigations; analyzing data Core ideaLS1.C Organization for matter and energy flow Crosscutting conceptEnergy and matter

Small disks are punched out of a spinach leaf and placed in a baking soda solution. The disks sink at first because their air spaces are filled with liquid. When the leaf photosynthesizes, it makes oxygen gas. The gas fills the air spaces and the disks float to the top. The faster photosynthesis happens, the sooner the disks float. The baking soda is the source of carbon dioxide (CO2) for the leaf.

Directions

  1. You will run an initial test and then 8 experiments, so 9 trials in all. Every trial uses 10 fresh disks and lasts up to 4 minutes.
  2. Before each trial, set up the lab exactly as the step says: drag the clamp lamp, choose the amount of baking soda, choose the light, and choose the water temperature. Start trial will not work until all four settings are right. Each experiment changes only one thing from the starting settings, so set everything else back to the starting settings.
  3. Press Start trial and watch the disks. The lab runs in real time. It cannot be sped up, slowed down, or paused. A beep sounds at the end of every minute. Count the disks that are floating and write the number on your own data table. Count a disk only when it reaches the surface.
  4. If all 10 disks reach the surface before 4 minutes, press Stop trial and write down the time on the clock. If they do not all float, the trial ends at 4:00 on its own. Record your final count.
  5. After all 9 trials, finish your graph and conclusion on your paper.

Each trial takes up to 4 minutes, so have your data table ready and turn your sound on so you can hear the minute beep.
